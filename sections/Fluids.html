Fluids ​

Bodies in RaiSim can float, sink, swim and fly through water and air. A world can hold volumes of liquid under a free surface, such as a pool, a lake or the sea, and an ambient fluid, such as air, everywhere else. Every dynamic body is held up by the weight of the fluid it displaces. The fluid drags it, lifts it, and carries part of its own mass along with it. Fluids act on single bodies, compounds, meshes and every link of an articulated system, through their collision shapes.

Fluids are off by default. A world without them steps exactly as before. The equations of the forces and of the motion they produce are written out in Dynamics of Fluid Forces.

Adding water and air ​

cpp
raisim::World world;

// air everywhere outside the water
world.setAmbientFluid(raisim::FluidMedium::air());

// a pool 4 m by 2.4 m, filled to 0.7 m
raisim::FluidVolume pool;
pool.medium = raisim::FluidMedium::water();
pool.surfaceHeight = 0.7;
pool.bottomHeight = 0.0;
pool.min = {-2.0, -1.2};
pool.max = {2.0, 1.2};
const size_t index = world.addFluidVolume(pool);

FluidMedium holds a fluid's density ρ, its dynamic viscosity μ and its velocity vf, which is a current or the wind. water(), seawater() and air() hold their values at 20 °C, 15 °C and 20 °C.

A FluidVolume holds the collision shapes whose centroids lie within its horizontal bounds and above bottomHeight, and that reach below surfaceHeight; by default it reaches the horizon. The surface cuts a shape where it crosses it. The walls and the bottom are meant to be solid, like the walls of a pool: a shape that reaches through them is still wholly in the liquid up to the surface. Where two volumes overlap, the first one added holds a shape.

To change the current, the water level or the bounds, call World::setFluidVolume(index, volume). Every call that changes a fluid wakes the sleeping bodies.

cpp
auto river = world.getFluidVolumes()[index];
river.medium.velocity = {0.5, 0.0, 0.0};
world.setFluidVolume(index, river);

Waves ​

A volume's waves move its surface and the liquid under it. Each FluidWave is a linear wave on deep water: it travels along its direction with its wavelength λ, and raises the surface by

η=acos⁡θ,θ=kd⋅x−Ωt+φ,k=2πλ,Ω=gk,

with its amplitude a and phase φ, at the world time t. The waves of a volume add up. A body sees the surface averaged over its extent: a plane with the height and tilt of the waves averaged over the body's horizontal spread, each wave weakened the more the longer the body is along it. A float heaves and rolls with the waves. A raft half a wave long feels the crest under one end and the trough under the other, with a buoyancy and moment within about 1 % of those of the water over its bottom in RaiSim's tests. A body small against the waves sees the plane tangent to them at its centroid. Under waves low against their length, the liquid moves in circles that shrink as e−kz with the depth z below the mean surface: forward under a crest, back under a trough. This motion adds to the current of the volume. A body is dragged along by it, and the liquid that accelerates pushes the body as it pushes the liquid the body displaces, and carries the body's added mass:

fa=(ρVs+MA)aw,

where aw is the acceleration of the liquid as it moves, which includes its change as it is carried from place to place. A particle as dense as the water therefore moves on the circle the water moves on, and over many waves it keeps its depth and drifts forward with them at the Stokes drift of low waves, a2Ωke−2kz. A body within the volume's horizontal bounds never falls asleep. rayrai and the TCP viewer draw the waves where the physics moves them, assuming g=9.81 m/s2.

cpp
raisim::FluidVolume sea;
sea.medium = raisim::FluidMedium::seawater();
sea.waves.push_back({{1.0, 0.0}, 20.0, 0.3, 0.0});  // direction, wavelength, amplitude, phase
sea.waves.push_back({{0.6, 0.8}, 7.0, 0.1, 1.0});
world.addFluidVolume(sea);

Rivers and other surfaces ​

A volume's sampler gives it a surface and a current that vary from place to place in any other way, and takes the place of its waves. It receives the centroid of a collision body and returns the height and normal of the surface over that point, and the velocity and acceleration of the liquid there. The body sees the surface as the plane through the sample, tilted by its normal, so it rides the slope of a wave. A wave field or a river simulated elsewhere plugs in this way. For example, rayrai's RayraiWindow::sampleWaterSurface() gives the waves it draws:

cpp
volume.sampler = [&viewer](const raisim::Vec<3>& point) {
  const auto surface = viewer.sampleWaterSurface({point[0], point[1]});
  raisim::FluidSample sample;
  sample.surfaceHeight = surface.height;
  sample.surfaceNormal = {surface.normal.x, surface.normal.y, surface.normal.z};
  return sample;
};

The sampler runs on the thread that steps the world, once for every collision body in the volume's bounds. The world stays deterministic as long as the sampler's result depends only on the point and the simulation state. A body within a sampled volume's horizontal bounds never falls asleep, wet or dry, since the surface or the current may change while it rests.

World files, scenes and Engine 2 ​

A world file holds the fluids in its fluids element and each body's coefficients in a fluid child (see World Configuration File); World::exportToXml() writes them. A .rscene scene holds them in its ambient_fluid and fluid_volume records and the fluid* keys of its objects (see .rscene File), and rayrai draws the water of its volumes. In RaiSim Engine 2, a FluidVolume node is a pool or the sea, and the Physics settings hold the ambient fluid; the Fluid Pool template starts from a pool with a current. Its exported world.xml carries the fluids to raisimGymTorch.

Buoyancy ​

The fluid pushes a body up with the weight of the fluid it displaces, and this force acts at the centroid of the displaced volume:

fb=−ρVsg.

Vs is the volume of the part of the shape below the surface. It is exact for every shape:

  • the cap of a sphere in closed form;
  • a box or a mesh by cutting its faces at the surface;
  • a cylinder or a capsule by integrating the circular segments under the surface along its axis.

Because the force acts at the centroid of the volume below the surface, the restoring moment of a floating body is that of hydrostatics. A body is stable where its metacenter lies above its center of mass. The half-dense wooden cube of the fluid pool example turns an edge up: a cube of density between 0.28 and 0.72 times that of water does not float on a face. The part of a body above the surface displaces the ambient fluid, so a light, large body in air also feels the air's buoyancy.

A body bobbing at the surface makes waves that carry its energy away, so it settles within a few periods. The volume's waveRadiation scales this damping, and 0 turns it off. At the body's natural heave frequency ωh, with waterplane area A, the damping is

b=bsbwbs+bw,bs=ρωh3A22g,bw=ρgA2ωh.

bs holds for a body small against the waves it makes (Haskind's relation for deep water). bw caps the damping for a wide body.

A body that falls into the water sets the water under it moving. As it sinks in, its added mass (see below) grows, and the body hands that new fluid its momentum (von Kármán's water entry): a flat plate that falls flat into the water slows at once to mv0/(m+mA).

Drag and lift ​

The surface of each shape is made of small faces. The flow pushes on a face that meets it with a pressure along its normal n, and rubs along the faces it passes:

f=−12ρA(Cp|w|max(0,w⋅n)n+Cf|wt|wt).

w is the velocity of the face relative to the fluid, wt its part along the face, and A the face's area. Integrated over a shape, the pressure gives the textbook drag 12ρCDAp|w|w on the area Ap the shape shows to the flow. Each shape takes its drag coefficient CD from measurements (Hoerner):

ShapeCD
SphereBrown and Lawler's correlation in the Reynolds number: 0.41 at 104, 0.47 at 105, Stokes' law below 1
Box face1.05
Cylinder across its axis1.0
Cylinder along its axis0.82
Capsule1.0 across its axis, the sphere's on its ends
Mesh triangle1.0

A plate that meets the flow at an angle of attack α feels the pressure on its lower face along its normal. This gives it lift as well as drag, CL=CDsin⁡αcos⁡α and CD(α)=CDsin2⁡α. A fin, a flipper or a leg sweeping through water pushes the body this way. Because the faces move with the body, a body that turns meets the flow faster on its outer faces. A long link that swings is braked as strip theory says. A sphere that spins feels only its skin friction, plus its Magnus lift, f=CMρV(ω×w). Cylinders and capsules feel Magnus lift from their spin about their axis.

Every shape also feels the Stokes drag of the viscosity, 3πμdw, and the Stokes torque πμd3ω of a sphere of the same volume, with diameter d. They dominate at low Reynolds number, where a grain settles at Stokes' speed. Drag and skin friction are applied implicitly: a light body in a viscous fluid settles stably even when the drag would stop it within a fraction of a time step, and a thin rod sliding along its length through water slows down without turning around.

Added mass ​

A body that accelerates in a fluid also accelerates the fluid around it, so it moves as if it were heavier. The extra inertia MA depends on the shape and the direction:

  • half the displaced mass for a sphere;
  • the displaced mass for a long cylinder moving across its axis;
  • far more than its own mass for a thin plate moving across its face.

RaiSim adds MA to the inertia of each body for the whole step. Contacts, joints, rolling and spinning friction and tendons therefore see it too, and a body much lighter than the water it displaces stays stable. A ping-pong ball let go under water starts up at 15.5 m/s², as it does in a tank. Without its added mass it would start at 112 m/s².

ShapeAdded mass
Sphere12ρV in every direction
EllipsoidLamb's coefficients (matches DNV-RP-C205, Table D-2, to 0.1 %)
BoxDNV-RP-C205 plates and prisms: 0.68ρa3 for a cube, 0.579ρπ4a3 for a square plate
Cylinderacross its axis within 5 % of DNV-RP-C205; along it, its flat ends
Capsule, meshthe ellipsoid that shows the same projected areas, at the shape's volume

The fluid carried along also turns the body as it moves (Kirchhoff's equations). A long body that moves at an angle to its axis feels the Munk moment, which turns it toward broadside to its motion. A body wholly in one still, inviscid fluid, with no other force on it, keeps its kinetic energy with its added mass exactly. Part of a body above the surface carries the added mass of the fluid there.

Fluid coefficients ​

FluidCoefficients sets how fluids act on a collision body:

FieldDefaultMeaning
enabledtruewhether fluids act on the shape
buoyancytruewhether the fluid holds it up
drag−1CD on the projected area; negative takes the shape's own (table above)
skinFriction0.005Cf of the flow along the surface
magnusLift0.5CM of a spinning sphere, cylinder or capsule
addedMass1scale of the added mass
cpp
raisim::FluidCoefficients hull;
hull.drag = 0.6;
world.setFluidCoefficients(boat, hull);          // every collision body of an object
robot->getCollisionBody("fin/0").setFluidCoefficients(hull);   // one body of an articulated system

Object::getFluidForce(localIdx) and Object::getFluidTorque(localIdx) return what the fluids did to a body in the last step, about its center of mass in the world frame. This covers buoyancy, drag, lift and the turn the added mass gives a body that moves, such as the Munk moment, at the velocity the step started with. The added mass that resists acceleration is not part of it.

Limits ​

  • The fluid does not move. Bodies do not stir it, so they leave no wake. A body in another body's wake, or between two walls, feels the free stream.
  • A body sees the surface as one plane: under waves, the waves averaged over its extent; under a sampler, the plane through the sample at its centroid, tilted by the sampler's normal. Waves that wet a body along part of its length and leave the rest dry, as over a raft thinner than they are high, are seen through that one plane, so its buoyancy is approximate. The velocity and acceleration of the waves are taken at the centroid, so a body longer than about a quarter of a wave feels their motion approximately.
  • A floating body drifts over many waves mostly because of the waves it reflects, which these forces leave out, so its drift is not reliable. A body under the surface drifts with the Stokes drift.
  • Forces are quasi-steady. The added mass of a body that pierces the surface is that of the part below it, which is approximate within a factor of order one at most wave frequencies.
  • Deformable and granular objects do not feel these fluids. Cloth has its own airDrag.
  • An articulated system integrating with EULER drifts off the orbits of the waves over many periods; SEMI_IMPLICIT (the default) and TRAPEZOID keep them.
  • The TCP viewer draws the water of every volume without a sampler as a still pool or sea, and the water of a scene file it receives in that scene's look. The waves of a sampled volume are not drawn there; rayrai draws them with RayraiWindow::setWaterBody().

Examples ​

  • Fluid pool: bodies of different densities float, tilt and sink.
  • Fluid stream: a stream, whose speed a slider in the viewer sets, carries crates, logs and balls off and slides bricks along its bed when strong.
  • Fluid stream with ANYmal: a stream strong enough tips a standing ANYmal over and carries it off.
  • Floating on waves: crates, a raft and barrels ride the swell rayrai draws, through a sampler.
  • Underwater ROV: a vehicle holds its station against a turning current.
