Linear elasticity validation and benchmarks ​

The new solver is written for RaiSim; MFEM and scikit-fem are used independently for validation. The scripts and raw measurements are in benchmark/ and benchmark/results/fem/ in the source repository.

Setup ​

Measurements used an AMD Ryzen 9 3950X, Linux x86-64, Clang 20 Release (-O3), with one solver thread and OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1. Build with 12 jobs. This is a shared development machine, not an isolated performance lab; repeat the measurements on your target system.

Each mesh is the same 1 × 0.25 × 0.25 m box, split into linear tetrahedra. Supports allow Poisson contraction while removing rigid modes; end traction is 1 MPa. The reference sweep includes Poisson ratios 0, 0.3, 0.45 and homogeneous or layered Young's modulus (70/210 GPa), at 1,536, 12,288 and 41,472 tetrahedra. MFEM receives exactly the same nodes and tetrahedra. The scikit-fem driver constructs the same topology and uses SciPy SuperLU. All runs check numerical agreement or analytical axial extension and independent equilibrium residuals.

Dependencies tested: Gmsh 4.15.2 (fixture generation only), MFEM 4.8 (a01719101027383954b69af1777dc828bf795d62), scikit-fem 12.0.2, SciPy 1.18.1, NumPy 2.5.4. MFEM is built serially, without MPI, METIS, OpenMP or external AMG; its comparison uses CG with symmetric Gauss-Seidel at relative tolerance 1e-11. This benchmark does not measure all MFEM backends or make a claim against AMG, PETSc, parallel or high-order FEM.

Multigrid preconditioner ​

Since 2026-11-09 the Automatic backend preconditions CG with smoothed-aggregation algebraic multigrid instead of incomplete Cholesky. Both stop at the same relative residual (1e-11), and the cantilever results below agree to ten digits. Measured on an Intel Core Ultra 7 265K pinned to one core, Clang 20 Release; times are cold solves in milliseconds (assembly, preconditioner setup, solve and recovery).

Multigrid iteration counts stay nearly constant as the mesh grows, while ICCG's grow with it. Bending and nearly incompressible material are where ICCG is slow. On a 52,000-tetrahedron STEP part refined adaptively to 394,000 tetrahedra (258,000 unknowns), one solve took 3.4 s and 62 iterations with multigrid against 60 s and 3,868 iterations with ICCG.

The ordering experiment compared Eigen IC with AMD and natural ordering. Natural ordering reduced setup and solve costs on this structured mesh sweep. An initial shift of 0.01 reduced measured IC setup time at high Poisson ratio; the final code uses natural ordering and that initial shift. Raw before/after and tuning measurements are retained alongside the final results. Lower-triangle sparse storage is retained for memory efficiency. A faster analytical principal stress eigenvalue routine was rejected after near-repeated eigenvalues lost accuracy; recovery still uses the robust eigensolver.

MFEM warm solve times omit stress recovery. Its changing-load sweep uses an absolute residual tolerance of 1e-13 times the RHS norm to resolve small transverse loads on top of the larger axial load. RaiSim includes all field recovery. The scikit-fem and RaiSim transverse sweeps have different small load amplitudes, so their changing-load timings are not identical-workload speed ratios. Direct solvers can be faster for many unrelated right-hand sides, despite higher setup cost; use SparseLDLT explicitly for that workload.

Imported mesh ordering check ​

A second fixture is a STEP solid with a cylindrical bore. Gmsh 4.15.2 imports the STEP geometry and generates 11,002 unstructured tetrahedra with 2,814 nodes and 8,094 free DOFs. The benchmark preserves Gmsh's node numbering, clamps the minimum-X end, and applies the same axial/transverse traction at the maximum-X end. Each row starts from a fresh solver. All cases converged; the largest load-relative force residual was below 2e-11. These results support keeping natural ordering and shift 0.01 across this structured/unstructured sample. They do not establish universal optimality. Node ordering, geometry, material contrast, and host load can change the result; repeat on representative production meshes. The initial shift is used only if the unshifted IC attempt fails; it changes the preconditioner, not the physical stiffness. Raw CSVs also retain iteration counts and separate setup/solve costs.

Bending convergence ​

linear_fem_bending measures a 1 × 0.1 × 0.1 m clamped cantilever with E=70 GPa and a uniformly distributed 1 kN transverse end load. It compares area-averaged tip displacement with Timoshenko beam theory, including Cowper's rectangular shear correction10(1+nu)/(12+11nu). Each longitudinal cell is approximately as long as a cross-section cell is wide (20/40/80 cells along the length). Beam theory is an engineering reference; it does not resolve the clamp and end load boundary layers of three-dimensional elasticity.

All six cases underpredict deflection. Refinement improves both materials, but linear tetrahedra remain much too stiff near incompressibility. Agreement with another T4 solver establishes implementation consistency, not continuum accuracy. Check mesh convergence for bending and local stress peaks. The workbench warns above Poisson ratio 0.45. Quadratic elements and mixed incompressible formulations require additional development.

Reproduce ​

Build linear_fem_benchmark and run it with no argument for all three sizes, or pass one subdivision count (4, 8, 12). The MFEM comparison is optional:

bash
cmake -S . -B /tmp/fem-build -DCMAKE_CXX_COMPILER=clang++-20 \
  -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DRAISIM_BENCHMARK=ON -DRAISIM_FEM_MFEM_BENCHMARK=ON \
  -DMFEM_DIR=/path/to/mfem-build
cmake --build /tmp/fem-build --target linear_fem_benchmark linear_fem_bending linear_fem_mesh_benchmark linear_fem_mfem -j12

Correctness checks ​

Unit tests cover affine patches, uniaxial extension, engineering shear, hydrostatic pressure, reaction forces and moments, gravity, heterogeneous materials, exact prescribed and multipoint constraints, cache changes and failed solve recovery, invalid/degenerate/unsupported meshes and mechanisms, import formats, overflow and locale independence. Fixture tests include axial, torsional and bending bolt response, nominal preload, connector rigid-motion invariance, bearing center restraints and free roll, remote motion, elastic foundations, nonmatching ties and RFEM round trips.

Fifteen independent scikit-fem/SciPy comparisons exercise distorted and rotated meshes, 1e-3/1e3 geometry scaling, negative/positive Poisson ratios, heterogeneous materials, prescribed motion, body and surface loads, and KKT reference solutions for multipoint constraints. They compare displacement, reaction, engineering strain, stress, principal stresses, von Mises and total strain energy. The MFEM sweep checks 18 additional material/mesh combinations; initial relative displacement differences were below 3e-12.

Regression tests also cover large unloaded/loaded slider mechanisms, edge and vertex hinges, weak or incomplete elastic supports, nonzero spring-rest motion, axis-equation fast paths, skew ICCG with gravity, independent response recycling, density-only cache edits, bolt stiffness changes and weighted nonrigid patches, sharp roller corners, connected/smooth picking, exact restored regions and legacy project compatibility.

The desktop and real Chrome/WebGPU tests additionally exercise file drops, project loading and solving, tetrahedral volume import, surface meshing, STEP translation, invalid-file errors and rendering. Browser workers are isolated from the rendering thread and can be cancelled.

Native meshing ​

The workbench meshes with RaiSim's constrained Delaunay mesher, natively and in the browser. The surface is remeshed to the element size and the CAD curvature with every CAD face boundary kept; points are inserted with exact predicates, the surface is recovered in the tetrahedralization, and the volume is refined and optimized. Max element edge is a hard bound. Automatic surface error is min(0.01 * edge, 0.001 * geometry_diagonal).

Measurements on 2026-11-11: Intel Core Ultra 7 265K, one performance core, Clang 20 Release, deterministic mode. Times are three-run medians that include helper startup, CAD translation, meshing and output. CAD was still translated with Open CASCADE 7.7.2 then; see CAD translation for the original reader that replaced it. Raw data: benchmark/results/fem/delaunay-mesher.csv and delaunay-mesher-vs-ftetwild.csv in the RaiSim source checkout.

Volume errors are zero for the planar assembly, 0.11% for the hollow cylinder and 0.43% for the sphere (fTetWild: 0.002%, 0.10% and 0.27%). The Delaunay mesher places more elements on curved parts, because its surface follows the CAD within the surface error with curvature-sized triangles. Its 1st-percentile mean ratio is about 0.50 on all fixtures (fTetWild 0.43-0.56). On a 141,000-node robot knee mount from a STEP file (5 mm), meshing takes 3.7 s on one core and 3.4 s with all threads, compared with 141 s for fTetWild.

Narrow CAD faces and sharp wedges are meshed with triangles sized to their width; folds are prevented, and tessellation defects from tiny CAD edges and folded triangles are repaired. benchmark/results/fem/step-corpus.csv meshed 68 public STEP models at three element sizes, 64 at every size, with the earlier Open CASCADE reader; it has not been repeated with the original reader.

Provided volume meshes use the independent refiner, retaining original vertices and region interfaces. Refining a 1 m cube from six supplied tetrahedra to maximum edges of 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 m takes 10, 23 and 113 ms, producing 6,144, 49,152 and 393,216 tetrahedra with exact volume and a minimum mean ratio of 0.762.

bash
python3 benchmark/fem_mesher_benchmark.py --mesher /path/to/raisim_fem_mesher \
  --geometry all --repetitions 3 --compare-mesher /optional/other/raisim_fem_mesher
python3 benchmark/fem_mesher_corpus_benchmark.py --mesher /path/to/raisim_fem_mesher \
  --folder /path/to/step/files

The benchmark reports timeouts explicitly and retains accuracy-target misses. The native geometry regression suite independently checks volume limits, positive tetrahedra, CAD units, separate solid regions, preservation of holes and the CAD repairs. Benchmark reporting tests cover accuracy misses, partial timeouts, empty output and changes to deterministic mesh output.
