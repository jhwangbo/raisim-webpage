Rayrai Example: Spherical Joint Friction ​

Overview ​

Coulomb friction of spherical joints (Joint Damping and Friction). Three spherical pendulums (m=1kg at d=0.5m) are released on the same conical swing, tilted 1.1rad, and differ only in the friction τc of their spherical joints:

Bob<dynamics friction>Friction coneWhat happens
bluenone–swings on
orange0.2Nm2.3°spirals down slowly and stops
red0.6Nm7.0°spirals down quickly and stops

The friction torque is the same in every direction and opposes the bob's rotation, so the swing loses energy at every turn instead of stopping one direction first. A bob can stop wherever gravity's torque, mgdsin⁡θ at the tilt θ, is at most τc: anywhere inside the ring drawn in its color. It stops off the vertical, and stays there. The example prints where and when each bob came to rest, and releases the pendulums again every 12 seconds.

Run ​

bash
./build-examples/examples/rayrai_spherical_joint_friction

CMake target: rayrai_spherical_joint_friction. Source: examples/src/rayrai/dynamics/rayrai_spherical_joint_friction.cpp. On Windows, run rayrai_spherical_joint_friction.exe instead.

API pattern ​

The friction is the standard URDF attribute of the spherical joint:

xml
<joint name="ball" type="spherical">
    <parent link="socket"/>
    <child link="bob"/>
    <dynamics friction="0.6"/>
</joint>

The joint's generalized velocity is the bob's angular velocity in its own frame. A joint held by friction keeps only round-off of it:

cpp
auto* pendulum = world->addArticulatedSystem(urdf);
pendulum->setState(gc, gv);  // gc: quaternion w, x, y, z; gv: angular velocity
world->integrate();
const bool atRest = pendulum->getGeneralizedVelocity().e().norm() < 1e-9;
