Server Example: Rocket Hop ​

A 627 kg rocket hops between two landing pads 20 m apart. It lifts off, climbs 25 m, flies across, descends, sets down on its legs at 0.35 m/s and cuts its engine. Three seconds later it flies back. It steers as launchers do, by tilting its engine on a gimbal, and four small thrusters at the top hold its roll.

The rocket ​

rsc/rocket/rocket.urdf describes a 5 m tank with a nose cone, four fins and four landing legs. The engine hangs from a two-axis gimbal at the bottom of the tank: the revolute joints gimbal_x and gimbal_y tilt it by up to 0.15 rad. The thrusters are URDF <thruster> elements:

xml
<thruster name="main_engine" link="engine" torque_coefficient="0.002">
    <origin xyz="0 0 0"/>
    <axis xyz="0 0 1"/>
    <limit lower="0" upper="12000"/>
</thruster>
<thruster name="rcs_roll_left_0" link="body">
    <origin xyz="0.36 0 3.95"/>
    <axis xyz="0 1 0"/>
    <limit lower="0" upper="60"/>
</thruster>
<!-- three more roll thrusters around the tank -->

The main engine sits on the engine link at the gimbal point and pushes along the nozzle, so tilting the engine tilts the thrust. Its torque_coefficient gives the rocket a roll torque of 0.002 N m per newton of thrust, as the swirl of an engine's exhaust does: about 12 N m in a hover. The roll thrusters push sideways at the edge of the tank, two for each direction of roll; each pair pushes in opposite directions on opposite sides, so it turns the rocket without moving it.

cpp
rocket->setThrust("main_engine", 6200.0);   // newtons; held until set again
rocket->setThrust(rollLeft, 17.0);
raisim::Vec<3> gimbal, axis;
rocket->getThrusterPose(rocket->getThrusterIndex("main_engine"), gimbal, axis);

Flying ​

Each flight follows four smooth segments for the rocket's center of mass: 25 m straight up in 9 s, across to the other pad in 9 s, and down in 9 s until it is 1 m above where it rests on the pad. It flies the last metre at a steady 0.35 m/s, as landers do, and cuts its engine as soon as the legs touch.

  • Thrust. The controller wants the acceleration of the segment plus a correction toward it. The thrust vector that gives it, m(a−g), sets where the rocket's axis should point (at most 15° from the vertical) and, projected on the axis, the engine's thrust. The engine cannot throttle below 40 %, 4.8 kN, so it is either off or between 4.8 and 12 kN; the rocket weighs 6.15 kN, so at the lowest throttle it descends.
  • Pitch and yaw. The engine thrust acts at the gimbal, 1.6 m below the center of mass. Tilting the engine by a small angle δ turns the rocket with a torque of about Tlδ, with the thrust T and the distance l. The controller tilts the engine to turn the rocket's axis toward the direction it wants. Servos hold the gimbal joints at the commanded angles.
  • Roll. The controller knows the engine's swirl from its torque_coefficient and the thrust, and has the roll thrusters cancel it and keep the fins on the world axes. They can only push, so one pair fires at a time.

The rocket tilts up to 0.23 rad when it starts across, and lands within 8 cm of the pad's center.

Build and run ​

CMake target: rocket_hop. Start rayrai_tcp_viewer, then the example:

bash
./build-examples/examples/rocket_hop

On Windows, run build-examples\bin\rocket_hop.exe. The viewer follows the rocket. The flame under the nozzle grows with the thrust, and a puff of gas shows each roll thruster that fires; both are visual objects of the example. The scene and the flight controller are in examples/include/rocket_hop.hpp.

Details ​

  • The rocket's mass is constant: it does not burn propellant.
  • The tank, its bands, fins and legs are URDF primitives; the nose cone and the bell nozzle are surfaces of revolution generated for this example. The four foot pads and the tank are the collision bodies.
  • The pads are static cylinders 5 m across and 0.2 m high.
  • See Thrusters and Grippers for the thruster model.
