Joint Damping and Friction ​

Every revolute, prismatic and spherical joint can have viscous damping and Coulomb friction. They model bearings, seals, gears and ball-and-socket joints. They are passive: they only remove energy, they are not part of the generalized force you set, and they act whether or not the joint is controlled.

Set them with the standard URDF <dynamics> attributes. Both default to zero:

xml
<joint name="knee" type="revolute">
    <parent link="thigh"/>
    <child link="shank"/>
    <axis xyz="0 1 0"/>
    <dynamics damping="0.5" friction="2.0"/>
</joint>

The MJCF loader reads the joint damping attribute but not frictionloss.

Both are measured at the joint, i.e., after any gear. If the joint is driven by an actuator, the actuator's output_damping and output_friction add to them (see Actuators).

Damping ​

Damping is the torque τd=−bu on the joint velocity u (relative to the parent body, see Joints). RaiSim integrates it implicitly with the trapezoidal rule, i.e., at the average velocity of the time step Δt:

τd=−but+ut+12.

For example, a free joint with inertia I under a constant torque τ and no gravity accelerates as

Iut+1−utΔt=τ−but+ut+12

and converges to the velocity τ/b. The implicit integration keeps a joint with a large damping stable at any time step. The d gain of the PD controller is integrated the same way. The difference is that damping pulls the velocity to zero, while the d gain pulls it to the velocity target.

To read the current joint damping, call getJointDamping(). It returns a const raisim::VecDyn reference in generalized velocity order, with getDOF() entries. These are the values loaded from the model or last supplied to setJointDamping(); actuator output damping and PD gains are separate. Copy getJointDamping().e() into an Eigen::VectorXd to preserve a baseline before adding temporary damping, such as wheel braking.

To change the damping at runtime, call setJointDamping() with one coefficient per degree of freedom (getDOF() entries). The new values act from the next step. Leave the six entries of a floating base at zero: they are not joints, and their damping would not be integrated implicitly.

The URDF effort limit bounds only the commanded torque (PD plus feedforward). The damping torque is passive and is never clipped. Actuator torques (see Actuators) are not bounded by the effort limit either: they are limited by the operating regions of their motors and are added after the effort clamp.

Friction ​

Friction is Coulomb friction with stiction. It opposes the motion with the constant torque τc, and it holds a joint at rest as long as the other torques on it (actuation, gravity, contacts and the motion of the other bodies) stay below τc:

τf=−τcsgn(ut+1) if ut+1≠0,|τf|≤τc if ut+1=0.

Once the other torques exceed τc, the joint accelerates with their sum minus τc. For the free joint above, that is I(ut+1−ut)/Δt=τ−τcsgn(ut+1).

RaiSim solves friction as a joint impulse bounded by τcΔt in the contact solver, together with the contacts and the joint limits. A joint therefore stops exactly instead of chattering around zero velocity, and the friction torque is consistent with the contact forces on the robot. A robot with friction adds one row to the contact solver; robots without friction do not pay for it.

Spherical joints ​

A spherical joint takes the same <dynamics> attributes:

xml
<joint name="shoulder" type="spherical">
    <parent link="torso"/>
    <child link="upper_arm"/>
    <dynamics damping="0.2" friction="1.5"/>
</joint>

The friction of a spherical joint (a ball-and-socket joint) acts on its three entries of the generalized velocity, uj∈R3: the angular velocity of the child body relative to the parent body (see Joints). Its friction torque τf∈R3 is bounded in magnitude by the same τc in every direction. It holds the joint at rest while the other torques on it stay below τc, and otherwise opposes the relative rotation with the full τc:

τf=−τcuj,t+1∥uj,t+1∥ if uj,t+1≠0,∥τf∥≤τc if uj,t+1=0.

The friction torque is opposite to the joint's angular velocity at the end of the step, whatever the inertia of the bodies, so it removes as much energy as the bound allows. Some consequences:

  • It is isotropic. A torque of 0.98τc is held in every direction, and a torque of 1.05τc turns the joint in every direction. The friction does not depend on the joint frame. Three revolute joints in a row (a gimbal), each with friction τc, behave differently. They hold up to 3τc about a diagonal, and their components stop at different times, which turns the axis of a rotation as it slows down.
  • A spin slows down about a fixed axis. A body with isotropic inertia I spinning about any axis slows down along that axis at the constant rate τc/I and stops.
  • A swing stops off the vertical. A pendulum of mass m whose center of mass is a distance d from the joint comes to rest wherever gravity's torque, mgdsin⁡θ at the tilt θ, is at most τc. The resting points form a cone around the vertical, as the figure shows. With only viscous damping, it would keep creeping toward the bottom.
  • A planar swing stays planar. A pendulum on a spherical joint whose mass is symmetric about its plane of swing, under loads in that plane, moves exactly like a revolute joint with the same friction about the plane's normal. (Without that symmetry, the body's own dynamics can turn it out of the plane, which a revolute joint would prevent.)

A spherical pendulum (m=1kg, d=0.5m, τc=0.6Nm) released at a tilt of 1.1rad on a conical swing. The bob's positions (light to dark) spiral down as friction removes energy, and the bob comes to rest 5.5° off the vertical, inside the ring where gravity's torque equals τc (a tilt of 7.0°). The Spherical Joint Friction example shows three such pendulums, with no, a little and a lot of friction.

Like the friction of a revolute or prismatic joint, it is solved together with the contacts, the joint limits and the other joints' friction. A contact pushing a link therefore cannot slip a joint unless the total torque on the joint exceeds τc. In a closed loop, the friction acts only in the directions the loop lets the joint turn. In the other directions the loop's forces alone carry the load, and they are reported in full. For example, if the link's tip is pinned, only twisting the link about its own axis meets the friction.

Accuracy ​

The friction of each joint is exact for the motion of the other joints and the contacts. When several joints with friction are strongly coupled, e.g., a long chain of spherical joints that mostly stick, the joints settle together over the contact solver's iterations. With the default solver settings the friction torque never exceeds τc, but a joint that should stick can keep creeping slowly, and a sliding joint can be braked by less than τc. With more solver iterations and a smaller tolerance (World::setContactSolverParam()), the friction converges to that tolerance.

Setting the friction ​

The joint's own friction is set in the model file; there is no C++ setter for it. The output_friction of an actuator can be changed at runtime by editing the definitions from getActuators() and passing them to setActuators().
