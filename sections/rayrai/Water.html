Water ​

rayrai draws oceans and rectangular bodies of water such as pools, ponds and tanks. The surface moves with trochoidal (Gerstner) waves, reflects the sky and the objects on screen, and refracts the scene below it. The water absorbs and scatters light, so a shallow floor shows through tinted and a deep one fades into the color of the water. Waves gather sunlight into caustics on the floor, and foam forms along shorelines and on compressed wave crests. A camera below the surface looks through murky water and up through Snell's window.

A rectangle can also be simulated as shallow water (see Flowing water): the water runs downhill over the scene under it, parts around rocks and bodies, and its ripples and foam drift with the current.

Water is drawn by every color render: the viewer window, renderWithExternalCamera, RGB sensors and reflection-probe captures. It is a visual only. It does not affect the physics, does not cast shadows, and is not seen by depth, segmentation or LiDAR renders. To let it act on bodies, query it (sampleWaterSurface, sampleWaterFlow) and apply the forces yourself.

Rayrai Example: Water Pond puts a pond in a meadow, with trees and a jetty on its banks and the sun glittering on the water. Rayrai Example: Water Stream runs a simulated creek down a hillside into a pool, with wooden balls floating down it.

Quick start ​

cpp
#include <rayrai/RayraiWindow.hpp>

auto world = std::make_shared<raisim::World>();
world->addGround(-10.0);                // sea floor
raisin::RayraiWindow viewer(world, 1280, 720);

raisin::WaterBody sea = raisin::defaultWaterBody(raisin::WaterPreset::Ocean);
sea.center = {0.0, 0.0, 0.0};          // mean sea level at z = 0
viewer.setWaterBody("sea", sea);

while (true) {
  world->integrate();
  // The surface moves only when its clock changes; follow the simulation time.
  viewer.setWaterTimeSeconds(world->getWorldTime());
  viewer.update(1280, 720, false, 0, 0, false);
}

setWaterBody(name, body) adds a body, or replaces the one with that name. removeWaterBody, clearWaterBodies, findWaterBody, waterBodyCount and waterBodyNames manage the list, and swapWorld clears it. An invalid body (out-of-range field, more than 32 waves, an empty name) throws std::invalid_argument and is not stored. validateWaterBody(body) runs the same check without a window and names the first invalid field.

defaultWaterBody(preset) gives a starting point:

PresetShapeLook
OceanoceanDeep blue sea, 16 wind waves from 0.8 m to 24 m long and up to 0.3 m high.
CalmLakeoceanGreen-blue lake water with small waves.
Pool10 m x 5 m rectangle, 1.5 m deepClear water, slight ripples, strong caustics.
MurkyPondoceanBrown-green turbid water that hides the floor within about a metre.

Shape and placement ​

WaterBody::shape is Ocean or Rectangle.

  • Ocean: an infinite plane at height center.z that reaches the horizon. Its surface is drawn well past the camera's far plane.
  • Rectangle: the surface spans halfExtents around (center.x, center.y), turned by yawRadians about +Z, and the water fills the box down to depth below it. The walls and the floor are your own scene geometry; set depth to the depth of the basin. Waves die out within edgeFadeDistance of the edges, so the surface meets the walls at its rest level.

Wave directions are given in the body frame: the world frame for an ocean, the turned rectangle frame for a rectangle.

Waves ​

The surface is a sum of up to 32 Gerstner waves (WaterBody::waves). In a Gerstner wave, each point of the surface moves on a circle as the wave passes. Wave i has:

  • a travel direction (normalized, in the body frame),
  • a wavelength λ (crest to crest, metres),
  • an amplitude A (crest height above the mean level),
  • a steepness in [0, 1],
  • a phase offset.

With k = 2π/λ, a point that rests at horizontal position p rises to A cos θ and moves horizontally by −(steepness/k) sin θ along the travel direction, where θ = k (d·p) − ω t + phase.

Waves travel at the deep-water speed: ω = sqrt(g k), so a 10 m wave moves at 4 m/s and a 1 m wave at 1.25 m/s. The horizontal motion gathers the points at the crests and spreads them in the troughs. Steepness 0 gives a sine wave; steepness 1 gives a cusped crest.

Where crests whose steepness adds up to more than 1 meet, the surface folds over itself. Folding cannot happen when the steepness of all waves adds up to 1 or less.

generateGerstnerWaves(WaterWaveSpectrum) creates a wind-driven set of waveCount waves (1 to 32, 16 by default), longest wave first:

  • Direction: the first wave travels along windAngleRadians. The others are turned by random angles within directionSpreadRadians.
  • Wavelength: decreases from maxWavelength to minWavelength along the list.
  • Amplitude: decreases from maxAmplitude to minAmplitude.
  • Steepness: rises from longWaveSteepness to shortWaveSteepness.
  • Shape of the decrease: the exponents wavelengthFalloff, amplitudeFalloff and steepnessFalloff set how quickly each quantity changes; larger values give more short waves.
  • Jitter: randomness (0 to 1) jitters each wave's place in the list, so the wavelengths are not evenly spaced.
  • Steepness cap: the steepness is scaled down so that the sum stays within maxSteepnessSum, which is 1 by default, so a generated surface never folds.

The same seed always gives the same waves on every platform.

Water time ​

The surface depends only on the water time, which changes only through setWaterTimeSeconds and advanceWaterTime. A render at a given water time is therefore repeatable. To keep the water in step with the simulation, set it from the world time:

cpp
viewer.setWaterTimeSeconds(world.getWorldTime());

waterTimeSeconds() returns the current water time. Phases are evaluated in double precision, so the surface stays smooth after days of water time.

Querying the surface ​

sampleWaterSurface(body, worldXY, timeSeconds) returns the surface above a horizontal position. It finds the rest point that the waves carry to that position, so it reports the surface exactly as it is drawn. The sample holds:

  • height: the world z of the surface;
  • normal: the surface normal;
  • compression: the horizontal area of the surface relative to its rest area, below 1 at crests;
  • inside: false outside a rectangle.

maxWaterWaveHeight(body) returns the largest height of the surface above its mean level, the sum of the wave amplitudes, for example to keep props or cameras clear of the crests.

RayraiWindow::sampleWaterSurface(worldXY) samples all bodies at the current water time and returns the highest surface. Use it to place floating props, to switch effects when the camera dips under water, or to compute buoyancy forces yourself.

How water looks ​

The water is modelled as a medium that absorbs and scatters light. The two coefficients are given per metre in linear RGB:

  • absorption removes light. Pure water absorbs red within a few metres and blue only over tens of metres, which is why clear water is blue.
  • scattering redirects light. It makes the water glow in its own color and look milky when strong.

The floor seen through water is dimmed by exp(−(absorption + scattering) × path), once on the light's way down and once on the view's way back up. Light scattered toward the camera gives deep water its color. That light comes from the sun and the sky entering through the surface, weighted by the Henyey-Greenstein phase function with scatteringAnisotropy g. Light scattered more than once is included approximately.

Absorption length, the depth over which light falls to 37%, is 1/absorption. A few reference values:

Waterabsorption (1/m, R G B)scattering (1/m, R G B)
Clear ocean (Ocean preset)0.45 0.07 0.0350.02 0.045 0.07
Swimming pool (Pool)0.38 0.055 0.020.002 0.003 0.004
Lake (CalmLake)0.55 0.11 0.200.06 0.10 0.07
Turbid pond (MurkyPond)0.9 0.7 1.30.35 0.42 0.22

The surface reflects and refracts by Fresnel's law for indexOfRefraction (1.333 for water):

  • Reflection: about 2% of the light at normal incidence, rising to everything at grazing angles. The sky is reflected everywhere. Objects visible on screen are reflected too (waterScreenSpaceReflections). Objects off screen show only the sky behind them.
  • Refraction: the scene below bends at the surface. An object that sticks out of the water in front of it is not pulled into the refraction.
  • Roughness: roughness sets the microfacet roughness at rest. Waves and ripples too fine for a pixel widen the reflections of the sun and sky at a distance instead of flickering.

Small ripples that are too short for the wave list come from a tiling ripple pattern (rippleStrength, rippleScale, rippleDirection). It drifts at the speed of real ripples of its size.

Foam, caustics and shorelines ​

  • Shoreline foam: where the water is shallower than shorelineFoamDepth, foam lines the shore and every object that pierces the surface.
  • Crest foam: where a crest compresses the surface below crestFoamThreshold of its rest area, foam covers it (crestFoamStrength).
  • Foam color: foamColor is the albedo of all foam, shoreline, crest and flowing, in linear RGB.
  • Caustics: causticStrength is the fraction of the sunlight reaching the floor that the waves gather into bright caustic lines. Only surfaces that face the sun and are not in its shadow receive them, and they fade with depth. causticScale sets their size.
  • Shoreline fade: shorelineFadeDepth fades the reflections in over the first centimetres of depth, so the water meets the shore without a hard edge.

Under water ​

The camera is under water when it is below the surface above it, inside a rectangle's footprint and above its bottom. An ocean has no bottom. Then the whole view is seen through the water:

  • The view itself: objects fade with distance into the scattered light. The scattered light is brighter near the surface and toward the sun.
  • Snell's window: looking up, the sky and the world above water show through a circle about 97° across.
  • Outside the window: the surface reflects everything (total internal reflection), so it mirrors the underwater surroundings.
  • At the waterline: the eye is either above or below the surface. A camera 1 cm above the water sees the surface from above, even where it passes in front of the near plane.
  • Looking into a tank from outside: a camera beside a rectangle sees the surface through only the water inside it. Objects inside the tank seen through its glass wall are not dimmed by the water, since nothing marks where the wall is.

waterDiagnostics().cameraUnderwater reports this state.

The underwater view is independent of the underwaterEnabled post-process overlay. That overlay tints the whole image regardless of any water body.

Flowing water ​

WaterBody::flow turns a rectangle into a body of simulated shallow water. The water runs downhill over the scene below the rectangle, fills hollows, flows around rocks, piers and banks, and leaves through open edges. RaiSim bodies that move push it aside and leave wakes. The surface is drawn from the simulated depth and velocity: its ripples, foam and caustics drift with the current.

cpp
raisin::WaterBody creek = raisin::defaultWaterBody(raisin::WaterPreset::CalmLake);
creek.shape = raisin::WaterBodyShape::Rectangle;
creek.center = {0.0, 0.0, -3.2};     // below the terrain: the creek starts dry
creek.halfExtents = {19.0f, 6.0f};
creek.depth = 0.5f;
creek.waves.clear();
creek.flow.enabled = true;
creek.flow.cellSize = 0.08f;         // 475 x 150 cells
creek.flow.bedCeiling = 6.0f;        // the terrain reaches up to 6 m above center.z
creek.flow.manningRoughness = 0.035f;

raisin::WaterFlowSource spring;
spring.position = {-17.5, -1.2};     // world XY
spring.radius = 0.5f;
spring.flowRate = 0.18f;             // m^3/s
spring.velocity = {0.8f, 0.0f};      // m/s
creek.flow.sources.push_back(spring);
viewer.setWaterBody("creek", creek);

while (true) {
  world->integrate();
  viewer.setWaterTimeSeconds(world->getWorldTime());   // the water advances with its clock
  viewer.update(1280, 720, false, 0, 0, false);
}

The dynamics ​

The water is described by its depth h and its depth-averaged horizontal velocity v over a bed of height zb. Mass and momentum are conserved:

∂h∂t+∇⋅(hv)=s,∂(hv)∂t+∇⋅(hvv⊤)=−gh∇(h+zb)−gn2|v|h1/3v+s+vs+s−v.
  • Gravity pushes the water down the slope of its surface h+zb. Still water over any bed, around islands and against banks, stays still and level.
  • Waves travel at gh relative to the current: 2 m/s in 0.4 m of water.
  • Bed friction follows Manning's law with roughness n (manningRoughness). On a long slope S the water settles where friction balances gravity; in a wide channel it flows at |v|=h2/3S1/2/n. The example creek carries 0.18 m³/s down a 7.5 % slope with n = 0.035 in a channel about 1.5 m wide, up to 16 cm deep at about 1.8 m/s.
  • Sources add water at s per unit area (WaterFlowSource, at most 16 per body), s+=max(s,0) and s−=min(s,0). A source is a disc: every cell whose center lies in it gets an equal share of flowRate, or the cell under its center when the disc holds no cell center. Water entering brings the source's velocity vs; a drain takes the water above it with its own velocity, never more than is there, so it lowers the water without speeding it up.
  • Shorelines move: water runs onto dry ground and recedes from it. A cell holding less than 1 mm counts as dry.

The total volume changes only by what the sources add and drain and what leaves through open edges. Foam forms where the water runs fast and shallow (Froude number well above one, such as over a drop), drifts with the current and fades within about a second (foamStrength).

The bed ​

The rectangle is divided into cells of about cellSize. The bed of each cell is the highest surface below it, seen from center.z + bedCeiling down to center.z - depth:

  • RaiSim objects, visuals and instanced visuals that cast shadows form the bed. Foliage that sways in the wind does not.
  • A solid that rises through bedCeiling (a pier, a bank) blocks the water. Anything wholly above it, such as a bridge deck or a canopy, does not, and the water flows under it.
  • RaiSim bodies that are not static are captured every frame while any part of them is in the water (movingBodies). A moving body pushes the water it takes up to the cells around it, which raises a bow wave ahead of it and leaves a trough behind it; the volume is kept.

The rest of the bed is captured when the simulation starts and again when objects or visuals are added or removed, or a static RaiSim object moves or changes its body type. A visual that moves is not followed; use a kinematic RaiSim body for an obstacle that moves. resetWaterFlow(name) starts the body over. At the start, every cell whose bed lies below center.z fills with still water up to center.z, so a center.z below the terrain starts dry. edges makes the rectangle's edges Open (the water leaves as if the bed continued beyond them) or Closed (walls).

Time ​

The simulation advances with the water clock (setWaterTimeSeconds, advanceWaterTime) when a color render finds the clock moved; renders at the same water time do not step it again. It takes as many steps as the fastest wave needs to stay stable (for water below the bed ceiling), at most 64 per render. If a frame needs more (after a stall), the rest of the time is dropped and reported in waterDiagnostics().flowTimeDropped. flowSteps reports the steps of the last render.

Querying the flow ​

sampleWaterFlow(worldXY) returns the simulated water above a horizontal position:

FieldMeaning
insideThe position lies in a simulated body whose state has been read back.
wetThe water is deeper than 1 mm.
surfaceHeightWorld z of the surface (of the bed where dry).
bedHeightWorld z of the bed, without the moving bodies.
depthWater depth.
velocityDepth-averaged horizontal velocity in the world frame.
timeSecondsWater time of the state sampled.

The state is copied back from the GPU after a render, so it trails the drawn water by one or two frames. Where a moving body stands or floats in the water, the sample reports the water around it: the surface and velocity of the nearest water no moving body covers, and the depth of that surface over the bed below the body. That is the water the body sits in, so it can be used for buoyancy and drag. The creek example applies to each floating ball:

cpp
const auto water = viewer.sampleWaterFlow({p[0], p[1]});
// Volume of the ball below the surface, and the velocity of the ball relative to the water.
const double submerged = std::clamp(water.surfaceHeight - (p[2] - r), 0.0, 2 * r);
const double volume = std::numbers::pi * submerged * submerged * (3 * r - submerged) / 3;
const Eigen::Vector3d relative(v[0] - water.velocity.x, v[1] - water.velocity.y, v[2]);
const double area = std::numbers::pi * r * r * submerged / (2 * r);
const Eigen::Vector3d drag = -0.5 * rho * cd * area * relative.norm() * relative;
ball->setExternalForce(0, drag + Eigen::Vector3d(0, 0, rho * g * volume));

Cost ​

The simulation runs on the GPU; its cost grows with the number of cells times the steps a frame takes, which grow with the speed of the fastest wave over the cell size. A 12 m x 12 m pool fed by a spring, with four piers and a moving box, at High and 1600 x 900 on an RTX 2070 SUPER (rayrai_benchmark --only=water_flow):

CellsFrameAdded by the simulation
plain water, not simulated1.9 ms
10 cm (120 x 120)2.6 msabout 0.7 ms
5 cm (240 x 240)3.5 msabout 1.6 ms
5 cm, movingBodies off3.2 msabout 1.3 ms

Limitations ​

  • Water is visual: it applies no buoyancy or drag. Use sampleWaterSurface or sampleWaterFlow to compute those yourself.
  • Only rectangles can be simulated, with at most 1024 cells along a side. The simulated water is one layer: a floating body counts as a column down to the bed, so the water flows around it, not under it, and water that falls over a cliff runs down its face rather than through the air. Moving bodies above one another count as one column.
  • Where two solids that rise through bedCeiling overlap, the overlap counts as open bed, so water can pass through a wall built of overlapping segments there. Let such pieces touch instead of overlapping, or keep their tops below bedCeiling.
  • The water must stay below center.z + bedCeiling: a source that keeps filling a closed basin above it is not supported.
  • On a simulated body, sampleWaterSurface and the underwater view use the simulated surface without the Gerstner waves drawn on it.
  • Depth, segmentation and LiDAR renders do not see water, and water casts no shadows.
  • Reflections of objects come from the screen. An object outside the image, or hidden behind another, is not reflected; the sky is reflected instead.
  • Waves do not feel the floor. They keep their height up to the shore of an ocean and up to the edge band of a rectangle.
  • Transparent objects, coordinate frames, point clouds and precipitation are drawn after the water. Below the surface they are hidden from a camera above it, and they are not dimmed by the water around a camera below it.
  • Fog on water follows the full PBR and mesh shaders. Plain PBR visuals drawn by the compact PBR shader use a slightly different fog curve, so in dense fog they can fade a little differently from the water beside them.
  • Water bodies are not part of .rscene files and are not sent to the TCP viewer.
